### Physics Subtopics — Detailed

**Ch 1–2: Basic Maths + Vectors**
- Differentiation and integration (kinematics application only); scalar vs vector; dot/cross products; unit vectors; resolution of components; angle between vectors

**Ch 2: Units and Measurements**
- SI system; dimensional analysis; significant figures; errors (absolute, relative, percentage); Vernier callipers; screw gauge

**Ch 3–4: Kinematics**
- Equations of motion (v=u+at; s=ut+½at²; v²=u²+2as); position/velocity/acceleration graphs; relative velocity; projectile motion (R=u²sin2θ/g; H=u²sin²θ/2g; T=2usinθ/g); uniform circular motion; centripetal acceleration
- ❌ Deleted: Integration-based kinematic derivations

**Ch 5: Laws of Motion**
- Newton's 3 laws; impulse-momentum theorem; linear momentum conservation; friction (static, kinetic; rolling conceptual only); free body diagrams; banking of roads; Atwood machine; pulley systems

**Ch 6: Work, Energy and Power**
- Work-energy theorem; conservative vs non-conservative forces; spring PE (U=½kx²); elastic/inelastic collisions; coefficient of restitution; power (P=Fv); energy conservation

**Ch 7: Rotational Motion ⭐**
- Moment of inertia (I); parallel axis theorem (I=Icm+Md²); perpendicular axis theorem (Iz=Ix+Iy); MI of ring, disc, rod, solid sphere, hollow sphere, cylinder; torque (τ=Iα); angular momentum (L=Iω); conservation of L when τ=0; rotational KE=½Iω²
- ❌ Deleted: Rolling motion (detailed rolling without slipping calculations)

**Ch 8: Centre of Mass & Collisions**
- COM formula (xcm=Σmixi/Σmi); motion of COM; perfectly elastic and inelastic collisions; 2D collisions; coefficient of restitution

**Ch 9: Gravitation**
- Newton's law (F=Gm₁m₂/r²); variation of g with altitude, depth, latitude; escape velocity (ve=√2gR); orbital velocity (vo=√gR); geostationary satellite (T=24h; height ~36000km); Kepler's laws (statement + numericals); gravitational PE; binding energy of satellite
- ❌ Deleted: Compound pendulum

**Ch 10–11: Properties of Matter**
- Stress-strain curve; Young's modulus (Y=Stress/Strain); Bulk modulus; Shear modulus; Hooke's law
- ❌ Deleted: Poisson's ratio
- Pascal's law; Archimedes' principle; buoyancy; Bernoulli's equation (P+½ρv²+ρgh=constant); applications; viscosity; Stokes' law (F=6πηrv); terminal velocity; surface tension; capillary rise (h=2Tcosθ/rρg)

**Ch 12: Thermal Properties of Matter ➕**
- Thermal expansion: linear (ΔL=LαΔT); area (ΔA=AβΔT); volume (ΔV=VγΔT); γ=2β=3α
- Specific heat capacity; calorimetry; latent heat (L); Stefan-Boltzmann law (E=σT⁴); Wien's displacement law (λmaxT=b); Newton's law of cooling
- NOTE: Thermal Properties is a separate chapter from Thermodynamics — ensure both are covered

**Ch 13: Kinetic Theory of Gases**
- Postulates; rms speed (vrms=√3RT/M); mean speed (vmean=√8RT/πM); most probable speed (vmp=√2RT/M); degrees of freedom; equipartition of energy (each DoF = ½kT); mean free path; Avogadro number

**Ch 14: Thermodynamics**
- Zeroth, First, Second laws; ΔU, ΔH; processes: isothermal (ΔT=0; ΔU=0); adiabatic (Q=0; ΔU=W); isobaric (P constant); isochoric (V constant; W=0); Carnot engine (efficiency=1-T₂/T₁); heat pump; refrigerator COP; entropy (conceptual; ΔS=Q/T)

**Ch 15–16: Oscillations + Waves**
- SHM: x=Asinωt; v=Aωcosωt; a=−Aω²sinωt; T=2π√(m/k); energy (KE+PE=½mω²A²); simple pendulum (T=2π√L/g); spring systems (series: 1/k=1/k₁+1/k₂; parallel: k=k₁+k₂); damped/forced oscillations; resonance
- Wave equation (y=Asin(kx−ωt)); standing waves; nodes/antinodes; strings (f=n/2L√T/μ); open pipe (f=nv/2L); closed pipe (f=(2n-1)v/4L); beats (fb=|f₁−f₂|); Doppler effect (all cases: source moving, observer moving, both moving)

**Ch 17–18: Electrostatics ⭐**
- Coulomb's law (F=kq₁q₂/r²; k=9×10⁹); superposition; electric field (E=F/q); Gauss's law (∮E·dA=q/ε₀); applications: sphere (E=kq/r² outside; 0 inside cavity); wire (E=λ/2πε₀r); infinite sheet (E=σ/2ε₀); dipole (p=qd; E at axial=2kp/r³; at equatorial=kp/r³); electric potential (V=kq/r); equipotential surfaces; conductors; dielectrics; capacitance (C=Q/V; parallel plate C=ε₀A/d; with dielectric C=κε₀A/d); capacitors series/parallel; energy stored (U=½CV²=Q²/2C); van de Graaff generator

**Ch 19: Current Electricity ⭐⭐**
- Drift velocity (vd=eEτ/m); Ohm's law; resistivity; temperature dependence (ρ=ρ₀(1+αΔT)); EMF; internal resistance (ε=V+Ir); Kirchhoff's laws (KCL: ΣI=0; KVL: ΣV=0); Wheatstone bridge (P/Q=R/S balanced); meter bridge; potentiometer (for EMF comparison and internal resistance); Joule heating (H=I²Rt); resistor colour code; cells in series/parallel

**Ch 20–23: Magnetism + EMI + AC ⭐**
- Biot-Savart law (dB=μ₀Idl×r/4πr³); Ampere's law; magnetic field: straight wire (B=μ₀I/2πr); circular loop; solenoid (B=μ₀nI); Lorentz force (F=qv×B); cyclotron; moving coil galvanometer; Earth's magnetism; dia/para/ferromagnetism; Curie's law (χ=C/T)
- Faraday's law (ε=−dΦ/dt); Lenz's law; motional EMF (ε=Blv); self-inductance (e=−LdI/dt); mutual inductance; energy stored in inductor (U=½LI²)
- LCR circuits: XL=ωL; XC=1/ωC; Z=√(R²+(XL−XC)²); resonance (XL=XC; fr=1/2π√LC); power factor (cosφ=R/Z); transformer (Ns/Np=Vs/Vp=Ip/Is)

**Ch 24: EM Waves**
- Displacement current (Id=ε₀dΦE/dt); Maxwell's equations (conceptual); EM spectrum (γ→X-ray→UV→Visible→IR→Microwave→Radio): frequency, wavelength, properties, uses; speed c=3×10⁸ m/s

**Ch 25–26: Optics ⭐**
- Reflection: mirror formula (1/v+1/u=1/f; f=R/2); magnification (m=−v/u); sign convention
- Snell's law (n₁sinθ₁=n₂sinθ₂); TIR (critical angle sinC=n₂/n₁); optical fibre; lens formula (1/v−1/u=1/f); lensmaker's equation; power (P=1/f in metres); lens combination (P=P₁+P₂); prism (δm=r+e−A; at min deviation A=2r); dispersion; scattering (Rayleigh: blue sky, red sunset)
- YDSE: fringe width β=λD/d; path difference Δx=dsinθ; bright (Δx=nλ); dark (Δx=(2n-1)λ/2); single slit diffraction (asinθ=mλ for minima); resolving power; Brewster's law (tanθB=n); Malus's law (I=I₀cos²θ)

**Ch 27–29: Modern Physics ⭐**
- Photoelectric effect: Einstein's equation (eVs=hν−φ; Vs=stopping potential; φ=work function); threshold frequency (ν₀=φ/h); slope of Vs vs ν = h/e; de Broglie wavelength (λ=h/mv=h/√2mKE)
- Bohr model: En=−13.6Z²/n² eV; rn=0.529n²/Z Å; vn=2.18×10⁶ Z/n m/s; spectral series (Lyman in UV; Balmer in visible; Paschen, Brackett, Pfund in IR)
- Nuclear: mass defect (Δm); binding energy (BE=Δmc²); BE per nucleon curve (Fe-56 most stable); radioactivity (N=N₀e^(−λt)); t½=0.693/λ; mean life τ=1/λ=1.44t½; α, β, γ decay; fission; fusion; nuclear reactor

**Ch 30: Semiconductors**
- Energy bands: conductor, semiconductor, insulator; intrinsic vs extrinsic (n-type: donor; p-type: acceptor); p-n junction; depletion layer; forward/reverse bias; I-V characteristics; rectifier (half-wave, full-wave); Zener diode (voltage regulation); BJT (npn/pnp; CE configuration; amplifier + switch); logic gates truth tables: AND, OR, NOT, NAND (universal), NOR (universal); Boolean algebra; De Morgan's theorems

**Ch 5: Salt Analysis**

**Ch 1: IUPAC Naming** — Rules for all functional groups; priority order (COOH > CHO > CO > OH > NH₂ > double bond > triple bond); cyclic and spiro compounds; common names of NEET-frequent compounds

**Ch 2: Isomerism**
- Structural: chain, position, functional group, metamerism, tautomerism (keto-enol; amino-imino)
- Stereoisomerism: Geometric (E/Z; cis/trans; conditions for geometric isomerism); Optical: chirality, chiral centre, enantiomers, diastereomers, meso compounds; R/S CIP priority rules; specific rotation; racemic mixture; resolution

**Ch 3: GOC ⭐**
- Homolytic/heterolytic fission; carbocation stability (3°>2°>1°>methyl; resonance stabilised most stable); carbanion stability (reverse order); free radical (planar, sp²); inductive effect (+I, −I groups); resonance; hyperconjugation (σ-conjugation; alkyl groups stabilise carbocations); electromeric effect (+E, −E); nucleophile vs electrophile; types of reactions (addition, elimination, substitution, rearrangement)

**Ch 4: Hydrocarbons ⭐⭐**
- Alkanes: free radical substitution mechanism (initiation → propagation → termination); stability order of radicals; halogenation selectivity
- Alkenes: Markovnikov's rule; peroxide effect (anti-Markovnikov; free radical mechanism); ozonolysis (O₃/Zn+H₂O → aldehydes; O₃/H₂O₂ → acids); oxidation (KMnO₄ cold dilute → diol; hot conc → oxidative cleavage); addition reactions (HX, H₂O, H₂, X₂, HOX)
- Alkynes: acidic character (terminal H; forms acetylide with NaNH₂); Markovnikov addition (water → ketone; Kucherov reaction); trimerization to benzene
- Benzene: aromaticity (Hückel's 4n+2; planar; cyclic; conjugated); EAS mechanism (halogenation/FeCl₃, nitration/HNO₃+H₂SO₄, sulphonation/H₂SO₄ fuming, Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation/AlCl₃); o/p directors (−NH₂, −OH, −OR, −R, halogens — activate except halogens which weakly deactivate) vs m-directors (−NO₂, −CN, −COOH, −SO₃H, −CHO — all deactivate)

**Ch 5: Haloalkanes + Haloarenes**
- SN1 (first order; tertiary; racemization; carbocation intermediate; polar protic solvent) vs SN2 (second order; primary; Walden inversion; backside attack; polar aprotic solvent)
- E1 vs E2; Saytzeff's rule (more substituted alkene preferred)
- Optical activity; DDT (health effects); freons (Cl → ozone depletion); iodoform test (CH₃CO— or CH₃CH(OH)— → CHI₃ yellow ppt)
- Haloarenes: nucleophilic substitution (harsh conditions; Meisenheimer complex); diazonium salts

**Ch 6: Alcohols, Phenols, Ethers**
- Lucas test (HCl/ZnCl₂; 3°=immediate turbidity; 2°=5 min; 1°=no reaction at room temp); Victor Meyer test
- Dehydration mechanism (acid-catalysed; primary→E2; tertiary→E1); order: 3°>2°>1°
- Phenol: Kolbe's reaction (CO₂/NaOH→salicylic acid); Reimer-Tiemann reaction (CHCl₃/NaOH→ortho-hydroxybenzaldehyde); reactions with FeCl₃ (violet colour)
- Williamson's synthesis (SN2; secondary and tertiary alkyl halides don't work due to elimination)
- Phenol acidity vs alcohol acidity (phenol more acidic due to resonance stabilisation of phenoxide)

**Ch 8: Organic Nitrogen Compounds ⭐**
- Amines: classification (primary, secondary, tertiary); basicity (alkyl>aniline; tertiary<secondary in aqueous due to solvation); Gabriel synthesis (phthalimide + KOH + RX → primary amine); Hofmann bromamide degradation (RCONH₂ + Br₂/KOH → RNH₂; carbon lost); carbylamine test (primary amine + CHCl₃/KOH → isocyanide; foul smell); diazotization (ArNH₂ + NaNO₂/HCl, 0-5°C → ArN₂⁺Cl⁻)

**Ch 9: Biomolecules**
- Carbohydrates: classification (mono, di, polysaccharides); mutarotation; reducing (free anomeric OH) vs non-reducing (no free anomeric OH; sucrose non-reducing); anomers (α and β); glucose: open chain (Haworth projection); cyclic (pyranose ring); Benedict's and Fehling's test; starch (amylose + amylopectin); glycogen; cellulose

**Ch 4 (XII): Principles of Inheritance and Variation ⭐⭐**
- Mendel's laws: Law of Dominance; Law of Segregation (Law of Purity of Gametes — alleles separate during gamete formation); Law of Independent Assortment (genes on different chromosomes)
- Monohybrid: F2 phenotypic ratio 3:1; genotypic 1:2:1; test cross 1:1
- Dihybrid: F2 9:3:3:1; independent assortment; 16 combinations
- Deviations from Mendel: incomplete dominance (1:2:1; snapdragon Antirrhinum); codominance (ABO blood groups; both alleles expressed); multiple alleles (ABO: IA, IB, i; 3 alleles, 1 individual has 2); epistasis (interaction between non-allelic genes; different ratios: 9:7; 12:3:1; 9:3:4; 15:1); pleiotropy (one gene → multiple effects; sickle cell anaemia); polygenic inheritance (many genes → one trait; skin colour, height)
- Linkage: genes on same chromosome; complete linkage (Drosophila male); incomplete linkage + crossing over; Morgan; recombination frequency (RF=recombinants/total offspring ×100%; 1% RF=1 cM=1 map unit)
- Sex determination: XX-XY (humans, Drosophila; male heterogametic); ZZ-ZW (birds, moths; female heterogametic); XO-XX (grasshopper; males XO); haplodiploid (honeybee; drone from unfertilised egg=haploid; worker/queen from fertilised=diploid)
- Sex-linked inheritance: X-linked recessive (colour blindness: X^B=normal, X^b=colour blind; haemophilia A: factor VIII; B: factor IX); criss-cross inheritance (trait passed from father to daughter to grandson)
- Pedigree analysis: square=male, circle=female, filled=affected, half-filled=carrier; autosomal dominant/recessive patterns; X-linked patterns
- Mutation: point mutation (substitution: missense, nonsense, silent; insertion/deletion → frameshift); chromosomal aberrations (structural: deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation; numerical: aneuploidy, polyploidy)
- Chromosomal disorders: Down syndrome (trisomy 21; 47 chromosomes; epicanthal fold; intellectual disability; congenital heart defects); Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY; male; small testes; sterile; gynaecomastia); Turner syndrome (45,XO or 45,X; female; webbed neck; short stature; sterile; no Barr body; horseshoe kidney); Superwoman (47,XXX; usually normal)

**Ch 5 (XII): Molecular Basis of Inheritance ⭐⭐⭐ — HIGHEST PRIORITY**
- Evidence: Griffith's transformation (1928; R strain → S strain virulence); Avery-MacLeod-McCarty (1944; DNA is the transforming principle); Hershey-Chase (1952; ³²P labels DNA, ³⁵S labels protein; phage DNA enters bacterium; confirmed DNA is genetic material)
- DNA structure: nucleotide = phosphate + pentose sugar + nitrogenous base; phosphodiester bond (3'→5' direction); antiparallel strands; base pairing (A=T via 2 H-bonds; G≡C via 3 H-bonds; Chargaff's rules A=T, G=C); B-form right-handed double helix; 3.4 Å per bp; 34 Å (3.4 nm) per turn; 10 bp per turn; width 2 nm
- DNA packaging: nucleosome (147 bp DNA wrapped ~1.65 times around histone octamer: 2 each of H2A, H2B, H3, H4; H1 linker histone seals DNA; "beads on a string"); 30 nm fibre → loops → higher-order coiling → metaphase chromosome
- DNA replication: semi-conservative (Meselson-Stahl experiment, 1958; ¹⁵N/¹⁴N CsCl density gradient); origin of replication (ori); helicase (unwinds); SSB proteins (stabilise); topoisomerase (relieves tension); primase (synthesises RNA primer); DNA pol III (main enzyme; 5'→3' only; requires primer); leading strand (continuous); lagging strand (Okazaki fragments; discontinuous; 150–200 bp each); DNA pol I (removes RNA primer; replaces with DNA); DNA ligase (seals nicks); replication is bidirectional in eukaryotes
- Transcription prokaryotic: template strand (3'→5'); RNA pol; sigma factor recognises promoter (-10 TATATA/Pribnow box, -35 TTGACA); rho-dependent (rho helicase unwinds; releases RNA) and rho-independent termination (hairpin loop); mRNA is directly translated (coupled transcription-translation)
- Transcription eukaryotic: RNA pol I (rRNA 28S, 18S, 5.8S); RNA pol II (hnRNA → mRNA); RNA pol III (tRNA, 5S rRNA); processing: 5' capping (7-methylguanosine cap — protects from exonucleases); 3' polyadenylation (poly-A tail, ~200A — stability, export); splicing (spliceosome removes introns, joins exons; snRNPs); monogenic mRNA
- Genetic code: triplet codons (3 nucleotides each); 64 total codons; 61 sense (specify amino acids); 3 stop codons (UAA = ochre; UAG = amber; UGA = opal; cause chain termination); AUG = START codon (codes for methionine; fMet in prokaryotes); degenerate (multiple codons for one AA; up to 6 for Ser, Leu, Arg); non-overlapping; commaless; nearly universal (except mitochondria); unambiguous (one codon → one AA)
- tRNA: cloverleaf structure; anticodon loop (complementary to codon; antiparallel, anticodon reads 3'→5' while codon goes 5'→3'); amino acid acceptor arm (3'-CCA-OH; amino acid attached to 3' end); aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase (charges tRNA; "second genetic code"); 61 sense codons but only 45 tRNAs (wobble base pairing at 3rd position)
- Translation: initiation (ribosome assembly at AUG; Shine-Dalgarno in prokaryotes); elongation (peptidyl transferase catalyses peptide bond; A site accepts new aminoacyl-tRNA; P site = peptidyl-tRNA; E site = exit; translocation moves ribosome 3 nt); termination (stop codon → release factors → polypeptide released; ribosome dissociates); polyribosomes (multiple ribosomes on same mRNA)
- Gene regulation: lac operon (inducible): lacZ (β-galactosidase), lacY (permease), lacA (transacetylase); repressor (lacI gene product) blocks operator; allolactose (inducer; causes conformational change in repressor → detaches from operator → transcription ON); catabolite repression (glucose present → low cAMP → CAP inactive → even with lactose, low transcription); trp operon (repressible): tryptophan (co-repressor) + aporepressor → active repressor → binds operator → transcription OFF; attenuation mechanism
- Human Genome Project: completed 2003; 3×10⁹ bp; ~20,000–25,000 genes; <2% codes for proteins; ~70% repetitive sequences; applications: medicine, anthropology, evolution; ethical/social/legal issues (ELSI programme)
- DNA fingerprinting: VNTR (Variable Number of Tandem Repeats; mini-satellites; highly variable between individuals); Southern blotting (gel electrophoresis of restriction-digested DNA → nitrocellulose membrane → hybridise with radioactive probe → autoradiograph); applications (forensic identification, paternity testing, evolutionary biology)

**Ch 6 (XII): Evolution ⭐⭐**
- Origin of life: Big Bang (~15 bya); Earth ~4.5 bya; early atmosphere reducing (CH₄, NH₃, H₂, H₂O; no O₂); Oparin-Haldane theory (coacervates = proto-life); Miller-Urey experiment (1953; inorganic molecules + electric discharge → amino acids; inorganic → organic synthesis confirmed); Fox's microspheres/proteinoids; RNA world hypothesis (RNA as first self-replicating molecule)
- Evidence: Fossils (palaeontology; relative dating = stratigraphy; absolute dating = radioactive decay); Comparative anatomy: homologous organs (forelimbs of whale, bat, cheetah, human — same structure/origin, different function; divergent evolution); analogous organs (wings of butterfly and bird — different origin, same function; convergent evolution); vestigial organs (appendix, nictitating membrane, ear muscles); Embryological evidence (gill slits in all vertebrate embryos; Ernst Haeckel's biogenetic law — ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny — discredited but useful); Biochemical evidence (cytochrome c sequence comparison; rDNA/rRNA phylogenetics)
- Theories: Lamarckism (use and disuse; inheritance of acquired characters; disproved — Weismann); Darwinism: natural selection based on overproduction, variation, struggle for existence, survival of fittest, descent with modification; limitations (didn't know mechanism of variation, heredity); Neo-Darwinism / Modern Synthetic Theory (combines Darwin + genetics; mutation is raw material; natural selection acts on genotypic variation)
- Mechanism of evolution: mutation (source of new alleles); natural selection (acts on phenotype); genetic drift (random allele frequency change; Founder effect — small group colonises new area; Bottleneck effect — population crash leaves few survivors); gene flow (migration; exchange of alleles between populations); isolation (geographic/reproductive)
- Types of natural selection: stabilising (intermediate phenotype favoured; reduces variation); directional (one extreme favoured; shifts population mean); disruptive (both extremes favoured; creates two sub-populations; leads to speciation)
- Hardy-Weinberg law: p+q=1; p²+2pq+q²=1 (p=freq of dominant allele; q=freq of recessive allele; p²=AA freq; 2pq=Aa freq; q²=aa freq); conditions for HWE (large population, random mating, no mutation, no selection, no migration); violation = evolution occurring
- Speciation: allopatric (geographic isolation → reproductive isolation → new species; Darwin's finches); sympatric (polyploidy in plants; habitat/temporal/behavioural isolation in same area); reproductive isolating mechanisms (pre-zygotic: habitat, temporal, behavioural, mechanical, gametic; post-zygotic: hybrid inviability, hybrid sterility, hybrid breakdown)
- Human evolution (chronology): Dryopithecus and Ramapithecus (~15 mya; ape-like); Australopithecus (~2 mya; Africa; walked upright; small brain); Homo habilis (~2 mya; first to use stone tools; brain ~650 cc); Homo erectus (~1.5 mya; first to leave Africa; fire; brain ~900 cc; Peking Man, Java Man); Homo sapiens neanderthalensis (~130,000–40,000 ya; Europe/Asia; large brain ~1400 cc; buried dead; culture); Cro-Magnon / Modern Homo sapiens (~30,000 ya onwards; cave art; brain ~1350 cc; cultural evolution)

**Ch 9 (XII): Biotechnology: Principles and Processes ⭐⭐⭐**
- Tools: Restriction endonucleases (RE; "molecular scissors"; recognise specific palindromic sequences 4–8 bp; EcoRI: G↓AATTC; BamHI: G↓GATCC; HindIII: A↓AGCTT; cut producing sticky ends or blunt ends); DNA ligase (joins sticky ends; "molecular glue"); vectors (plasmids: pBR322 — ori + ampR + tetR + MCS; insertional inactivation for blue-white screening; Ti plasmid from Agrobacterium for plant transformation; retroviruses, adenoviruses for animal/gene therapy)
- PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction): denature (94°C; H-bonds break; strands separate) → anneal (55°C; specific primers bind complementary sequences) → extend (72°C; Taq DNA polymerase — thermostable, from Thermus aquaticus — extends primer 5'→3'); exponential amplification (2ⁿ copies after n cycles); applications: forensics, disease diagnosis, research
- Cloning: insert gene into vector → recombinant DNA (rDNA) → transform into competent host (E. coli; CaCl₂ or electroporation); selectable markers (antibiotic resistance); screening: insertional inactivation (gene cloned into lacZ/ampR → disrupts it → different colony colour/antibiotic sensitivity); blue-white selection (X-gal chromogenic substrate; β-galactosidase produces blue → blue colony = no insert; white = insert)
- pBR322: 4361 bp; ori (replication origin); ampR (ampicillin resistance); tetR (tetracycline resistance); MCS within tetR (insertion inactivates tetR; amp-resistant but tet-sensitive = recombinant)
- Gel electrophoresis: separate DNA by size; agarose gel; electric field (−ve to +ve; DNA moves toward +ve); EtBr (ethidium bromide) stain; UV light visualisation; size marker/ladder

**Ch 10 (XII): Biotechnology and Its Applications ⭐⭐**
- Genetically modified organisms: Bt crops (Bt cotton — Cry1Ac against Helicoverpa armigera/bollworm; Cry2Ab against leaf worm; toxin protein encoded by cry gene; harmless to non-target organisms; activated at alkaline pH of insect midgut); Bt brinjal (Cry1Ac; approved in Bangladesh); Golden Rice (daffodil psy gene + bacterial crtI gene → β-carotene/pro-vitamin A in endosperm; addresses Vitamin A deficiency; not widely approved)
- RNAi (RNA interference/post-transcriptional gene silencing): dsRNA → RISC (RNA-induced silencing complex) → cleaved into siRNA (small interfering RNA) → guide strand targets complementary mRNA → degradation; applications: nematode resistance in tobacco (Meloidogyne incognita RNAi); insect resistance; gene expression studies
- Gene therapy: somatic (non-heritable; treat disease in individual) vs germline (heritable; ethical issues); ADA deficiency (adenosine deaminase; SCID — severe combined immunodeficiency; child in "bubble"; world's first approved gene therapy 1990; ADA gene introduced via retroviral vector into lymphocytes or stem cells; ADA enzyme therapy alternative); cf. cystic fibrosis, haemophilia, cancer
- Molecular diagnostics: ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay; antigen-antibody; detect HIV, hepatitis B, typhoid); PCR (amplify pathogen DNA; detect very early infection even before symptoms; HIV, tuberculosis, cancer mutations); Recombinant DNA probes (labelled; hybridise to specific sequences)
- Transgenic animals: oncomouse (predisposed to cancer; drug testing); pharming (production of human proteins in animal milk: Factor VIII in sheep, α-1-antitrypsin in sheep, insulin in pigs); xenotransplantation (pig organs with human surface proteins); vaccine testing; disease modelling (Alzheimer's, cystic fibrosis mouse models)
- Insulin production: human insulin (humulin) from E. coli (Eli Lilly, 1982; first commercial biotech product); A chain and B chain produced separately → combined; C-peptide removed (unlike natural insulin production via proinsulin); advantages over pig/bovine insulin (no allergic reactions; unlimited supply)
- Biopiracy: use of bio-resources by multinationals without compensating source country/community; examples: basmati rice patent (RiceTec USA; challenged by India); turmeric (wound healing; patent filed in USA; revoked after India provided prior art); neem (pesticidal properties; European patent; revoked); Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD 1992) and Nagoya Protocol address biopiracy

**Ch 11 (XII): Organisms and Populations**
- Ecology levels: organism → population → community → ecosystem → biome → biosphere
- Organisms and abiotic factors: temperature (poikilotherm vs homeotherm); water; light (photoperiodism; photosynthesis); soil (edaphic factors)
- Adaptations: thermal (Allen's rule — smaller extremities in cold); xerophytic (sunken stomata; thick cuticle; CAM); hydrophytic; high altitude; caves
- Population attributes: birth rate; death rate; sex ratio; age distribution (pre-reproductive, reproductive, post-reproductive); population density (number/area; biomass; cover; frequency)
- Population growth: Exponential (dN/dt=rN; J-curve; unlimited resources; r=intrinsic rate of natural increase); Logistic (dN/dt=rN[(K−N)/K]; S-curve/sigmoidal; K=carrying capacity; N=K/2 is point of maximum growth; realistic)
- r-strategists (r-selected): high r, short life, many small offspring, unstable environment (insects, small mammals); K-strategists (K-selected): low r, long life, few large offspring, stable environment (elephants, humans)
- Population interactions: mutualism (+/+; Rhizobium-legume; mycorrhiza; lichens); commensalism (+/0; barnacle on whale; orchid on tree); competition (−/−; interspecific: Gause's competitive exclusion principle — two species occupying same niche cannot coexist; resource partitioning); predation (+/−; lynx-hare; coevolution; camouflage; chemical defence — Monarch butterfly-cardiac glycosides); parasitism (+/−; Cuscuta on Acacia); amensalism (−/0; Penicillium vs bacteria)

**Ch 12 (XII): Ecosystem**
- Components: biotic (producers, consumers, decomposers) + abiotic (light, temperature, water, soil, minerals)
- Productivity: GPP (Gross Primary Productivity; total photosynthesis); NPP = GPP − R (R=respiration); Secondary productivity (by consumers); Standing crop (biomass at given time)
- Decomposition: steps: fragmentation (detritivores break up litter) → leaching (water-soluble nutrients move to soil) → catabolism (enzymatic breakdown by microbes) → humification (humus formation; resistant dark material) → mineralisation (inorganic nutrients released); rate depends on: temperature (warm faster), moisture (moist faster), chemical quality of detritus (low lignin/chitin = faster)
- Energy flow: unidirectional; not recycled; 10% law (Lindeman 1942): ~10% of energy transferred between trophic levels; 90% lost as heat, respiration, incomplete digestion; Sun → producers → herbivores → primary carnivores → secondary carnivores → tertiary carnivores
- Ecological pyramids: Numbers (individuals per trophic level; can be inverted — parasites on single tree); Biomass (dry weight per trophic level; usually upright; inverted in aquatic — phytoplankton turnover rapid, so standing crop < zooplankton); Energy (always upright; no exceptions; kcal/m²/yr)
- Succession: primary (bare rock/water/sand; pioneer species → climax community; takes thousands of years); secondary (after disturbance; faster; soil present); hydrosere (water → marsh → forest); xerosere (bare rock → grassland → forest); climax community (stable; in equilibrium with climate)
- Nutrient cycles: Carbon (fast cycle: photosynthesis/respiration; slow cycle: fossil fuels; atmosphere+oceans as reservoir); Nitrogen (N-fixation: Rhizobium/Azotobacter/lightning/Haber process → ammonification: decomposers break protein → NH₃ → nitrification: Nitrosomonas (NH₃→NO₂⁻) + Nitrobacter (NO₂⁻→NO₃⁻) → denitrification: Pseudomonas/Thiobacillus (NO₃⁻→N₂)); Phosphorus (sedimentary; no gaseous phase; weathering → plants → consumers → decomposers → back to soil/water)
- Ecosystem services: O₂ production; CO₂ sequestration; water purification; pollination; soil formation; nutrient cycling; flood control; climate regulation; timber; medicines; food

**Ch 13 (XII): Biodiversity and Conservation**
- Levels: Genetic (allelic variation within species; mango 1000+ varieties; human HLA diversity); Species (species richness; alpha diversity = within habitat; beta diversity = between habitats; gamma diversity = landscape); Ecological (community/ecosystem diversity)
- Patterns: latitudinal gradient (richness decreases from tropics to poles; tropics more productive, stable climate, more evolutionary time); species-area relationship (logS = logC + Z×logA; Z=0.1–0.2 for small areas; 0.6–1.2 for large landmasses/continents; slope Z reflects area sampled)
- Global biodiversity: ~8.7 million eukaryotic species estimated; 1.5 million formally described; ~70% of all species are insects; more than half of species in tropics; ~25% of drugs from plants
- Biodiversity loss — HIPPO: Habitat loss and fragmentation (most important; tropical deforestation; 1 hectare/second destroyed); Invasive species (Nile perch in Lake Victoria → extinction of 200 cichlid species; water hyacinth Eichhornia in Indian water bodies; Parthenium, Lantana, Ageratum in India; zebra mussels in Great Lakes); Pollution (pesticides, heavy metals, light, noise, plastic); Population growth and over-consumption; Overexploitation (whaling, bushmeat, overfishing)
- IUCN Red List categories: EX (Extinct), EW (Extinct in Wild), CR (Critically Endangered), EN (Endangered), VU (Vulnerable), NT (Near Threatened), LC (Least Concern), DD (Data Deficient)
- Biodiversity hotspots: 34 worldwide; criteria: >1500 endemic vascular plant species AND >70% original habitat already lost; India's 4 hotspots: Western Ghats + Sri Lanka; Eastern Himalaya; Indo-Burma; Sundaland (includes Andaman & Nicobar)
- In situ conservation: national parks (highest protection; no human activity; Corbett, Kaziranga); wildlife sanctuaries (some human activity allowed); biosphere reserves (core+buffer+transition zones; local communities); sacred groves (community-protected; Meghalaya, Rajasthan); Ramsar sites (wetlands of international importance; Chilika Lake); Marine Protected Areas (coral reefs)
- Ex situ conservation: zoos (AZA studbooks; captive breeding; reintroduction); botanical gardens (Kew, Royal Botanical Garden; living plant collections); seed banks (Svalbard Global Seed Vault, Norway; −18°C; long-term storage; National Bureau of Plant Genetic Resources, India); cryopreservation (liquid nitrogen −196°C; gametes, embryos, tissues); tissue culture (in vitro; important for endangered plants)
- International: Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD, 1992, Rio Earth Summit; 3 goals: conservation, sustainable use, equitable benefit sharing); CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species; controls trade; Appendix I = no trade); WWF; IUCN; Nagoya Protocol (access and benefit sharing; biopiracy)

**Ch 14 (XII): Environmental Issues ➕ (was missing — now included)**
- Air pollution: Primary pollutants (emitted directly): SO₂ (fossil fuel combustion; acid rain); NOₓ (vehicle exhaust; smog); CO (incomplete combustion; binds Hb → CO poisoning); SPM/PM₂.₅/PM₁₀ (suspended particulate matter; respiratory; cardiac effects); hydrocarbons (volatile organic compounds); Pb (leaded petrol — now phased out); Secondary pollutants (formed in atmosphere): O₃ (tropospheric; smog; irritant); PAN (peroxyacetyl nitrate; photochemical smog); sulphate + nitrate particles; acid rain; London smog vs LA photochemical smog
- Effects of air pollution: respiratory (bronchitis, asthma, emphysema, lung cancer); monument decay: "Black stone disease" / "stone leprosy" — SO₂ + marble (CaCO₃) → CaSO₄ (gypsum; weaker; washes off; Taj Mahal); crop damage; acid rain (pH<5.6; damages forests, corrodes, acidifies lakes; SO₂+H₂O→H₂SO₃; NO₂+H₂O→HNO₃)
- Greenhouse effect: natural (CO₂, H₂O vapour, CH₄, N₂O, O₃ trap longwave radiation); enhanced (increased GHGs from human activity → global warming); greenhouse gases: CO₂ (main; burning fossil fuels, deforestation); CH₄ (paddy fields, cattle, landfills; 25× more potent than CO₂ over 100 years); N₂O (fertilisers, livestock; 298×); CFCs (refrigerants, aerosols; also deplete ozone; 5000–14000×); consequences: glacial melt, sea level rise, changed precipitation patterns, coral bleaching, species extinction; Paris Agreement 2015 (limit warming to 1.5–2°C)
- Ozone depletion: stratospheric ozone (O₃ layer at 20–35 km altitude); natural cycle (O₂+UV → 2O; O+O₂ → O₃; O₃+UV → O₂+O; in balance without CFCs); CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons; Freons; released Cl radical in stratosphere: Cl+O₃→ClO+O₂; ClO+O→Cl+O₂; chain reaction — one Cl destroys thousands of O₃ molecules); Rowland and Molina (1974; Nobel 1995); Antarctic ozone hole (spring September-October; polar vortex; polar stratospheric clouds); UV-B increase → skin cancer (melanoma), cataracts, immune suppression, crop damage; Montreal Protocol 1987 (phase-out CFCs; most successful environmental treaty; replaced by HCFCs, HFCs — lower ODP but greenhouse gases)
- Water pollution: domestic sewage (BOD: Biochemical Oxygen Demand = O₂ needed by bacteria to decompose; high BOD = high pollution; clean river BOD <1 ppm; heavily polluted >5 ppm; coliform count); eutrophication (excess N, P from agricultural runoff/sewage → algal bloom → light blocked → algae die → decomposition by bacteria → O₂ depleted → anaerobic → fish kill; cultural eutrophication faster than natural); industrial (heavy metals: Minamata disease, Japan 1950s — Hg methylation by bacteria → bioaccumulation in fish → Minamata disease in humans (numbness, blindness, paralysis, birth defects); Itai-itai disease, Japan — cadmium from zinc mines → bone softening, kidney failure); thermal pollution (power plants release hot water → lowers dissolved O₂ → kills aquatic life); oil spills (coat animals, block photosynthesis)
- Sewage treatment: Primary (screening + settling → removes floating and settleable solids → primary sludge + clarified effluent); Secondary/biological (aerobic bacteria decompose organic matter; trickling filter or activated sludge process; BOD reduced by 80–90%; secondary sludge; effluent mostly treated; biogas from sludge); Tertiary (chemical precipitation of phosphates; nitrate removal by denitrification; chlorination/UV/ozone disinfection; removes most pathogens; expensive); effluent discharged into river must meet standards
- Solid waste management: Municipal (landfill: anaerobic, leachate issue, methane; sanitary landfill better; incineration: ash, air pollution; composting: organic fraction → humus; recycling: 3R — Reduce, Reuse, Recycle); biomedical waste (colour-coded bags: yellow=infectious/pathological/anatomical; red=contaminated recyclables; blue=glass/metallic sharp; black=cytotoxic; incineration or autoclaving); radioactive waste (low level: burial; high level: deep geological repository at stable rock formation); e-waste (computers, phones; Pb, Cd, Hg, Br; proper recycling; informal recycling in developing countries dangerous)
- Biomagnification (bioaccumulation/bioconcentration): persistent organic pollutants (DDT, PCBs, dioxins) — lipophilic, not excreted, accumulate in fatty tissues; concentration increases at each trophic level; highest in top predators; DDT in fish-eating birds (Peregrine falcon, osprey) → eggshell thinning (inhibits Ca²⁺ deposition) → reproductive failure; Arctic food chain even though no DDT used there; banned in most countries but persistent in environment; Great Lakes example
- Noise pollution: >80 dB (decibels) causes hearing damage; >140 dB = immediate pain; sources: traffic, industry, construction, aircraft, firecrackers; physiological effects: hearing loss, hypertension, stress, sleep disturbance; control: sound barriers, mufflers, earplugs, green belts (trees absorb), regulations
- Case studies: Chipko movement (1973; Chamoli district, Uttarakhand; Sunderlal Bahuguna, Chandi Prasad Bhatt, Gaura Devi; villagers hugged trees to prevent logging; influenced Forest Conservation Act 1980; "ecology is permanent economy"); JFM (Joint Forest Management: community + government partnership; 25% of forest produce to community; India 1988); Amrita Devi Bishnoi (1731; Khejarli village, Rajasthan; led 363 Bishnois who died hugging sacred Khejri trees Prosopis cineraria to stop logging by king's men; Bishnoi community principle: harm no living thing); Ramsar Convention on Wetlands (1971; Ramsar, Iran; wetlands of international importance; 169 countries; Chilika Lake, Keoladeo Ghana designated)

**Ch 1: The Living World**

**Ch 2: Biological Classification ⭐**
- Two kingdom system (Linnaeus; Plantae + Animalia; problems with fungi, bacteria); Five kingdom system (R.H. Whittaker, 1969; Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia; basis: complexity of cell, complexity of body, mode of nutrition, phylogenetic relationships)
- Monera: prokaryotes; no nuclear membrane; cell wall; includes bacteria + archaebacteria + cyanobacteria; Mycoplasma (no cell wall; smallest living cell); bacteria shapes: coccus, bacillus, vibrio, spirillum; heterotrophic (saprophytic/parasitic) or autotrophic (photosynthetic or chemosynthetic); reproduction: binary fission; can form endospores (resistant to harsh conditions)
- Protista: unicellular eukaryotes; autotrophic or heterotrophic; kingdom-level bridge; Chrysophytes (diatoms — silica cell wall "diatomite"; golden-brown; photosynthetic); Dinoflagellates (cellulose plates; bioluminescent; red tides); Euglenoids (Euglena; mixotrophic; pellicle not cell wall; freshwater); Slime moulds (Physarum; acellular; pseudoplasmodia); Protozoans: Amoeboid (Amoeba, Entamoeba); Flagellated (Trypanosoma sleeping sickness; Leishmania kala-azar); Ciliated (Paramecium; oral groove); Sporozoans (Plasmodium malaria)
- Fungi: eukaryotic; mostly multicellular (except yeast unicellular); heterotrophic (saprophytic or parasitic); cell wall of chitin; reserve food glycogen; mycelium (filaments = hyphae; septate or aseptate/coenocytic); reproduction (vegetative: fragmentation; asexual: spores conidia/sporangiospores/zoospores; sexual: ascospores/basidiospores/zygospores; sexual cycle: plasmogamy + karyogamy + meiosis); Classes: Phycomycetes (aseptate; zygospore; Mucor, Rhizopus, Albugo); Ascomycetes (septate; ascospores in asci; Aspergillus, Penicillium, Claviceps, Neurospora, morels/truffles, Saccharomyces); Basidiomycetes (septate; basidiospores; Agaricus mushroom, Ustilago smut, Puccinia rust); Deuteromycetes (imperfect fungi; no sexual stage; Alternaria, Colletotrichum, Trichoderma); Lichens (symbiosis: algae + fungi; pioneer species; pollution indicators)

**Ch 3: Plant Kingdom ⭐**
- Algae: thallophytes; aquatic; autotrophic; unicellular to multicellular; classification by pigment and reserve food: Chlorophyta (green; Chlamydomonas, Volvox, Ulva, Spirogyra; starch; chlorophyll a+b); Phaeophyta (brown; Fucus, Laminaria, Sargassum; mannitol+laminarin; fucoxanthin; alternation of generations); Rhodophyta (red; Polysiphonia, Porphyra, Gracilaria, Gelidium; floridean starch; phycoerythrin; found at depth)
- Alternation of Generations: gametophyte (n) → gametes → zygote (2n) → sporophyte (2n) → spores (n) → gametophyte; haplontic (dominant gametophyte: algae, bryophytes); diplontic (dominant sporophyte: gymnosperms, angiosperms); haplo-diplontic (both independent: Pteridophytes, some algae like Ectocarpus)
- Bryophytes ("amphibians of plant kingdom"): gametophyte dominant; no vascular tissue; water needed for reproduction; rhizoids; Liverworts (Marchantia; thalloid; asexual by gemmae in gemma cups; sexual by archegoniophore and antheridiophore); Mosses (Funaria; protonema stage; leafy gametophyte; sporophyte partially dependent)
- Pteridophytes: first vascular plants (xylem + phloem); sporophyte dominant; gametophyte (prothallus; independent; bisexual; free-living); requires water for fertilisation; homosporous vs heterosporous (micro/megaspores; heterosporous = step toward seed); Lycopsida (Selaginella, Lycopodium); Sphenopsida (Equisetum); Pteropsida (Pteris, Adiantum); Psilopsida (Psilotum)
- Gymnosperms ("naked seeds"): sporophyte dominant; seed but no fruit; microsporophylls (pollen) and megasporophylls (ovule); pollen tube; dominant in cold/arid; Cycas (dioecious; coralloid roots = Anabaena; male cone but NO female cone — only female shoot); Pinus (monoecious; winged seeds; resin ducts; wood = softwood); Gnetum; Sequoia (largest/tallest trees)
- Angiosperms: seeds enclosed in fruit; double fertilisation; most advanced; monocot vs dicot

**Ch 5: Morphology of Flowering Plants**
- Root: tap root (dicot; deep; Gram, Mustard) vs fibrous (monocot; superficial; Wheat, Paddy); adventitious roots (arise from non-root parts; Banyan aerial prop roots); modifications: storage (carrot taproot, Dahlia tuberous adventitious), respiratory (Rhizophora pneumatophores in mangroves), climbing (Piper betel), reproductive (Sweet potato); parts: root cap, meristematic zone, elongation zone, maturation zone (root hair zone)
- Stem: aerial (erect; branched; nodes and internodes) or underground modifications (rhizome horizontal: Ginger, Turmeric; corm vertical: Colocasia; tuber: Potato eyes = nodes with buds; bulb: Onion = fleshy leaves + reduced stem plate); aerial modifications: tendrils (Cucumber = stem; Pea = leaflet); thorns (Bougainvillea); runners (Strawberry); offsets (Pistia)
- Leaf: leaf base (pulvinus in some); petiole; lamina; venation (reticulate = dicot; parallel = monocot); phyllotaxy (alternate: China rose; opposite: Calotropis; whorled: Nerium); leaf modifications: tendrils (Pea leaflets; Gloriosa); spines (Cactus = modified leaves; Opuntia); phyllode (flattened petiole: Acacia); pitcher (Nepenthes leaf + lid); bladder (Utricularia)
- Inflorescence: racemose (main axis continues; flowers in acropetal succession; bottom-most oldest; raceme/spike/spadix/catkin/corymb/umbel/capitulum); cymose (main axis ends in flower; oldest flower at top; monochasial/dichasial/polychasial)
- Flower: complete (all 4 whorls) vs incomplete; bisexual (hermaphrodite) vs unisexual (monoecious: both sexes on same plant; dioecious: on different plants); symmetry (actinomorphic/regular: Mustard; zygomorphic/irregular: Pea, Cassia; asymmetric: Canna); aestivation (arrangement of petals in bud: valvate, twisted, imbricate, vexillary/papilionaceous); calyx (sepals; gamosepalous/polysepalous); corolla (petals; gamopetalous/polypetalous); androecium (stamens; monadelphous/diadelphous/polyadelphous; syngenesious=anthers fused: Compositae); gynoecium (pistil; monocarpellary/multicarpellary; syncarpous/apocarpous; ovule number); placentation (axile: Tomato, Lemon; parietal: Mustard; free central: Dianthus; basal: Sunflower; marginal: Pea)
- Fruit: simple (from single carpel or syncarpous gynoecium: berry=Tomato; drupe=Mango; pome=Apple false fruit); aggregate (from apocarpous gynoecium: Strawberry); composite/multiple (from inflorescence: Mulberry, Fig, Pineapple)
- Seed: dicot (testa+tegmen; hilum; micropyle; cotyledons=2; endospermic: Castor or non-endospermic: Gram, Pea); monocot (coleoptile protects plumule; coleorhiza protects radicle; endosperm large; aleurone layer; scutellum=single cotyledon)
- Important families: Fabaceae/Leguminosae (Pea; papilionaceous corolla; 10 stamens diadelphous 9+1; marginal placentation; legume/pod fruit); Solanaceae (Potato, Tomato, Tobacco; actinomorphic; 5 fused petals; 5 epipetalous stamens; bicarpellary syncarpous gynoecium; berry fruit); Liliaceae (monocot; Onion, Garlic, Aloe; trimerous; axile placentation; fruit = capsule or berry)

**Ch 6: Anatomy of Flowering Plants**
- Meristematic tissues: apical (root tip, shoot tip; primary growth); lateral (vascular cambium, cork cambium; secondary growth); intercalary (between mature tissues; grass nodes)
- Simple tissues: Parenchyma (thin wall; living; food/water storage; photosynthesis in mesophyll; aerenchyma in aquatic); Collenchyma (irregularly thickened corners; living; mechanical support in herbaceous stems: petiole of Cucurbita); Sclerenchyma (thick, lignified cell wall; dead; fibres or sclereids = stone cells; support and protection; fibres in jute/hemp; sclereids in nut shells, pear grit cells)
- Complex tissues: Xylem (dead at maturity except xylem parenchyma; tracheids, vessels, xylem fibres, xylem parenchyma; water + mineral conduction); Phloem (sieve tube elements with companion cells=living; phloem parenchyma; phloem fibres=bast; food conduction)
- Tissue systems: Epidermal (outermost; cuticle; stomata guard cells; trichomes); Ground (cortex, endodermis, pericycle, pith; mostly parenchyma in monocot); Vascular (xylem + phloem in bundles; conjoint: monocot collateral open or closed; dicot root — radial; stem — collateral open with cambium; leaf — vascular bundle with bundle sheath)
- Root anatomy: Dicot root — outermost epidermis (piliferous layer) → cortex (parenchyma) → endodermis (Casparian strips: suberin/lignin thickening on radial and transverse walls; controls ion entry) → pericycle (meristematic; origin of lateral roots) → vascular bundles (radial; xylem and phloem alternate; tetrarch/hexarch/polyarch); Monocot root — polyarch; conjunctive tissue between xylem and phloem
- Stem anatomy: Dicot stem — epidermis → cortex (hypodermis of collenchyma → parenchyma → endodermis with starch) → vascular bundles (conjoint, collateral, open; cambium strip between xylem and phloem; arranged in a ring; primary and secondary xylem/phloem) → pith (large parenchyma); Monocot stem — epidermis → hypodermis (sclerenchyma) → ground tissue (no distinct cortex/endodermis/pericycle) → vascular bundles (scattered; conjoint, collateral, closed = no cambium; bundle sheath of sclerenchyma)
- Secondary growth: vascular cambium (intrafascicular between xylem/phloem + interfascicular arising from parenchyma → complete ring) → secondary xylem (wood) inside + secondary phloem outside; annual rings (earlywood = spring wood, large vessels, less dense; latewood = autumn wood, small vessels, more dense); heartwood (old non-functional, dark; tyloses) vs sapwood (functional, light); cork cambium/phellogen → cork (phellem; suberin; dead; impermeable; replaces epidermis) + secondary cortex (phelloderm; living; inside phellogen); periderm = phellogen + phelloderm + phellem; lenticels (loosely arranged cells; gas exchange through bark)
- Leaf anatomy: Dorsiventral (dicot) — adaxial epidermis → palisade mesophyll (1-2 layers columnar chlorenchyma; main photosynthesis) → spongy mesophyll (irregular; large intercellular spaces; gas exchange) → abaxial epidermis; more stomata on lower surface; Isobilateral (monocot) — chlorenchyma not differentiated into palisade and spongy; equal stomata on both surfaces; vascular bundles with large and small bundles alternating; bundle sheath of large bundle = parenchyma with starch (not in C3)

**Ch 8: Cell: The Unit of Life ⭐**
- Cell theory (Schleiden, Schwann 1838–39; Virchow 1855 "omnis cellula e cellula"); prokaryotic vs eukaryotic
- Prokaryotic cell: no nuclear membrane; 70S ribosomes; no membrane-bound organelles; cell wall (bacteria: peptidoglycan/murein; archaebacteria: pseudopeptidoglycan or protein; cyanobacteria: gram negative); plasma membrane; capsule (polysaccharide; virulence, protection); flagellum (protein flagellin; rotary motor); fimbriae/pili (attachment; conjugation); nucleoid (naked circular DNA + plasmids); mesosomes (infoldings of plasma membrane; enzymes for cellular respiration; site of DNA replication and cell division)

**Ch 9: Biomolecules (Class XI)**
- Carbohydrates: monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose; 6-carbon; ribose/deoxyribose 5-carbon); disaccharides (sucrose = glucose+fructose; maltose = glucose+glucose; lactose = glucose+galactose; glycosidic bond; hydrolysis); polysaccharides (starch: amylose linear+amylopectin branched; glycogen: highly branched; cellulose: β-1,4 linkage; chitin: N-acetylglucosamine)
- Proteins: 20 amino acids (α-carbon with amino+carboxyl+R group; essential = 9); peptide bond (condensation; -CO-NH-); levels of structure (1°=sequence; 2°=H-bonds α-helix β-sheet; 3°=3D folding; 4°=subunit assembly); fibrous (collagen, keratin, myosin) vs globular (haemoglobin, enzymes, antibodies); enzymes (catalyst; Km=Michaelis constant=substrate conc at half-max velocity; Vmax; competitive inhibition increases Km; non-competitive decreases Vmax)
- Lipids: fatty acids (saturated=no double bonds; unsaturated=double bonds; cis=natural); triglycerides (glycerol+3 fatty acids; ester bonds; storage fat); phospholipids (glycerol+2 fatty acids+phosphate head; amphipathic; bilayer); sterols (cholesterol: membrane fluidity, steroid hormones); waxes (waterproofing)
- Nucleic acids: nucleoside = base+sugar; nucleotide = base+sugar+phosphate; DNA (A,T,G,C; deoxyribose; double-stranded); RNA (A,U,G,C; ribose; usually single-stranded); phosphodiester backbone

**Ch 10: Cell Cycle and Cell Division ⭐**
- Cell cycle: Interphase (G1 = growth, organelle synthesis, preparation; S = DNA replication, histone synthesis; G2 = further growth, protein synthesis, organelle duplication); M phase (actual division); G0 (quiescent phase; permanently differentiated cells exit cycle)
- Mitosis: prophase (chromatin condenses → chromosomes; centrioles move to poles; spindle forms; nuclear envelope breaks); metaphase (chromosomes align on equatorial plate; kinetochore microtubules attach; easiest to count chromosomes); anaphase (centromeres split; sister chromatids move to poles; cell elongates); telophase (nuclear envelope reforms; chromosomes decondense; nucleolus reappears); cytokinesis (animal: cleavage furrow; plant: cell plate from inside out; phragmoplast)
- Significance of mitosis: growth; repair; asexual reproduction; maintaining chromosome number; genetic identity
- Meiosis: only in germ cells; two sequential divisions; one DNA replication; 4 haploid cells
  - Meiosis I (reductional): Prophase I (LONGEST; 5 substages): Leptotene (chromosomes condense; become visible), Zygotene (homologous chromosomes pair = synapsis; bivalent/tetrad formation; synaptonemal complex), Pachytene (crossing over occurs here between non-sister chromatids; chiasmata form; recombination=new combinations), Diplotene (synaptonemal complex dissolves; chiasmata visible; chromosomes repel each other; held at chiasmata), Diakinesis (maximum condensation; chiasmata move to ends=terminalization; nuclear envelope breaks); Metaphase I (bivalents on equatorial plate); Anaphase I (homologues separate to poles; centromere does NOT split; each cell gets one homologue = reductional); Telophase I + cytokinesis → 2 haploid cells with sister chromatids still joined
  - Meiosis II (equational; like mitosis): Prophase II, Metaphase II, Anaphase II (centromere splits; sister chromatids separate), Telophase II → 4 haploid cells (spermatids or 1 egg + 3 polar bodies)
- Significance of meiosis: genetic recombination (crossing over); halving of chromosome number; variation = raw material for evolution; genetic diversity

**Ch 11: Transport in Plants**
- Diffusion: passive; no energy; down concentration gradient; short distances
- Facilitated diffusion: channel proteins; aquaporins (water); no energy; faster than simple diffusion
- Osmosis: movement of water across semipermeable membrane from low solute (high water potential ψ) to high solute (low ψ); water potential (ψ = ψs + ψp; ψs = solute potential, always negative; ψp = pressure potential, positive in turgid cell, negative in xylem); plasmolysis (loss of water → cytoplasm shrinks away from wall; deplasmolysis on returning to water); turgor (cell wall prevents bursting; turgor pressure = pressure potential)
- Active transport: against gradient; energy (ATP); carrier proteins; Na⁺-K⁺ pump example
- Water movement in root: apoplast pathway (through cell walls, intercellular spaces; faster; blocked at Casparian strips in endodermis); symplast pathway (through cytoplasm and plasmodesmata); transmembrane pathway; Casparian strips force water to enter symplast at endodermis (selective filtration)
- Ascent of sap: cohesion-tension theory (Dixon and Joly); transpiration pull creates tension; water molecules cohesive; adhesion to xylem walls; continuous water column from root to leaf (TACT: Transpiration pull, Adhesion, Cohesion, Tension); transpiration: stomatal (main; guard cells change shape by osmosis; K⁺ ion pumping; ABA closes stomata under stress); cuticular; lenticular
- Phloem transport (translocation): bidirectional; pressure flow hypothesis (Munch); source (high sugar → active loading → high osmotic pressure → water enters by osmosis → high pressure); sink (unloading → low pressure); sugar flows from high to low pressure in sieve tubes
- Mineral uptake: active (against gradient; energy); selective; root hairs have high surface area; mycorrhizal association enhances phosphate uptake

**Ch 12: Mineral Nutrition**
- Essential elements: 17 total; macronutrients (>10 mmol/kg; C, H, O, N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S); micronutrients/trace elements (<10 mmol/kg; Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, Mo, B, Cl, Ni); criteria: essential for growth/reproduction, cannot be replaced, directly involved in metabolism
- Functions: N (amino acids, proteins, nucleic acids, chlorophyll); P (ATP, nucleic acids, phospholipids, coenzymes); K (stomatal regulation, turgidity, enzyme activation); Ca (middle lamella pectin; cell division; signal transduction); Mg (chlorophyll centre; enzyme activator); Fe (redox reactions; nitrogen fixation; chlorophyll synthesis); Mn (photosystem II water splitting; enzyme activator)
- Deficiency symptoms: N → chlorosis (yellowing; older leaves first — mobile element); P → purpling of leaves; K → necrosis (scorched edges); Ca → tip die-back; Mg → interveinal chlorosis; Fe → interveinal chlorosis (young leaves; immobile)
- Nitrogen metabolism: nitrogen cycle; N-fixation (N₂→NH₃): biological (Rhizobium symbiotic in legume root nodules — leghaemoglobin keeps O₂ low for nitrogenase; Azotobacter, Anabaena free-living; lightning); nitrification (NH₃→NO₂→NO₃; Nitrosomonas, Nitrobacter); assimilation (NO₃ → reduced to NH₄⁺ → amino acids via GS/GOGAT); denitrification (NO₃→N₂; Pseudomonas); ammonification (proteins → NH₃ by decomposers)

**Ch 13: Photosynthesis ⭐⭐**
- Chloroplast: double membrane; stroma (dark reactions; ribosomes; DNA; starch grains); thylakoid (grana = stacked thylakoids; light reactions; pigments); chlorophyll a (primary pigment; all photosynthetic organisms; absorbs violet-blue and red; reflects green); chlorophyll b, carotenoids (accessory; absorb other wavelengths → transfer to chlorophyll a; also protect from photo-oxidation)
- Light reactions (in thylakoids): Photosystem II (P680; absorbs 680nm; water oxidation: 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂; O₂ is byproduct) → primary acceptor → plastoquinone (PQ) → cytochrome b6f complex → plastocyanin (PC) → Photosystem I (P700; 700nm; NADP⁺ reductase) → ferredoxin → NADP⁺ → NADPH; Cyclic photophosphorylation (only PS I; no water oxidation; no NADPH; only ATP; PS I → ferredoxin → cytochrome b6f → plastocyanin → back to PS I); Non-cyclic (Z-scheme; both PS I and II; produces ATP + NADPH + O₂); ATP synthesis = photophosphorylation (chemiosmosis; CF₀-CF₁ ATP synthase; proton gradient across thylakoid membrane)
- Calvin cycle (C3 pathway; in stroma): Carboxylation (CO₂ + RuBP 5C → RuBisCO → 2×3-PGA 3C; rate-limiting step; RuBisCO most abundant enzyme); Reduction (3-PGA + ATP + NADPH → G3P = glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate; 1 G3P out of 6 produced goes to synthesis; 5 go to regeneration); Regeneration (5 G3P + ATP → 3 RuBP; 5C; ready for next cycle); 3 turns for 1 molecule of G3P (net carbon gain)
- C4 pathway (Hatch-Slack pathway): adapted for hot dry tropical climates; Kranz anatomy (mesophyll + bundle sheath cells; both with chloroplasts; dense chloroplasts in BS; agranal/few grana in BS); CO₂ fixed by PEP carboxylase (PEPC; in mesophyll; much higher affinity for CO₂ than RuBisCO; can fix CO₂ at very low concentrations; no photorespiration) → OAA (4C) → malate → transported to bundle sheath → decarboxylated → CO₂ released → enters Calvin cycle in BS; examples: maize (Zea mays), sugarcane (Saccharum), sorghum, Amaranthus, Atriplex
- CAM (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism): desert succulents (cacti, pineapple, Agave); stomata open at NIGHT (prevents water loss) → CO₂ fixed by PEPC → malate stored in vacuole → day: stomata closed, malate decarboxylated → CO₂ enters Calvin cycle; temporal separation (night fixation, day Calvin) vs spatial separation in C4
- Photorespiration: RuBisCO adds O₂ instead of CO₂ when O₂ concentration high → wasteful; produces no ATP; no NADPH; releases CO₂; occurs in C3 plants; absent in C4 (CO₂ concentrating mechanism prevents it) and CAM; significance: consumes up to 25% of photosynthate in C3 plants
- Factors affecting photosynthesis: light (rate increases with intensity up to light saturation point); CO₂ concentration (major limiting factor at normal atmospheric levels); temperature (Topt 25–35°C; high temp denatures enzymes); water (substrate for light reactions; closure of stomata at low water → reduces CO₂ entry); Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors (rate of photosynthesis is limited by the slowest factor)

**Ch 14: Respiration in Plants ⭐**
- Glycolysis (EMP pathway; Embden-Meyerhof-Parnas): occurs in cytosol; anaerobic or aerobic; BOTH; glucose (6C) → 2 pyruvate (3C); steps (10 enzymes); energy cost: 2 ATP used; energy gained: 4 ATP + 2 NADH; Net: 2 ATP + 2 NADH + 2 pyruvate; key steps: glucose → glucose-6-phosphate (hexokinase); fructose-1,6-bisphosphate → 2 triose phosphates (aldolase); 2 NAD⁺ reduced to 2 NADH (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase); 2 ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation (phosphoglycerate kinase + pyruvate kinase)
- Fermentation (anaerobic): pyruvate → acetaldehyde + CO₂ (pyruvate decarboxylase) → ethanol + NADH oxidised (alcohol dehydrogenase) [yeast, facultative anaerobes]; OR pyruvate → lactate (lactate dehydrogenase; NADH oxidised) [RBCs, muscles under intense activity]; inefficient — only 2 ATP per glucose; toxic products
- Aerobic respiration: pyruvate enters mitochondria; pyruvate decarboxylation (pyruvate + CoA + NAD⁺ → acetyl CoA + CO₂ + NADH; pyruvate dehydrogenase complex in matrix)
- Krebs cycle (TCA/Citric Acid Cycle; in mitochondrial matrix): acetyl CoA (2C) + OAA (4C) → citrate (6C) → isocitrate → α-ketoglutarate + CO₂ (NADH) → succinyl CoA + CO₂ (NADH) → succinate (GTP) → fumarate (FADH₂) → malate → OAA (NADH); PER turn: 3 NADH + 1 FADH₂ + 1 GTP + 2 CO₂; 2 turns per glucose: 6 NADH + 2 FADH₂ + 2 GTP + 4 CO₂
- Electron Transport Chain (inner mitochondrial membrane): Complex I (NADH dehydrogenase; NADH → NAD⁺; pumps 4H⁺) → ubiquinone/CoQ → Complex II (FADH₂ → FAD; no H⁺ pumped) → ubiquinol → Complex III (cytochrome bc1; pumps 4H⁺) → cytochrome c → Complex IV (cytochrome c oxidase; 4H⁺ pumped; O₂ final acceptor; O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O); Proton gradient across inner membrane → ATP synthase (F₀F₁-ATPase; proton flow drives rotation → phosphorylation); P/O ratios: NADH → 2.5 ATP; FADH₂ → 1.5 ATP; Total from complete oxidation of 1 glucose: ~30–32 ATP (modern estimates; textbook may say 36–38 — use textbook in exam)

**Ch 15: Plant Growth and Development**
- Growth: irreversible increase in dry mass; meristematic regions; phases (meristematic, elongation, maturation); exponential (S-curve; sigmoid); measurement by area, dry weight, length
- Differentiation vs dedifferentiation (re-acquire meristematic activity) vs redifferentiation
- Development: totipotency (any somatic cell can develop into complete plant; demonstrated by tissue culture)
- Plant growth regulators (PGRs): Auxin (IAA; produced at shoot apex; promotes cell elongation; apical dominance; phototropism; geotropism; rooting; fruit setting; synthetic 2,4-D = herbicide for broad-leaved weeds; IBA for adventitious roots); Gibberellins (GA₃; cell elongation in stem; overcoming dormancy; bolting; fruit setting in seedless grapes; aleurone layer activation in germination); Cytokinins (zeatin; promote cell division; delay senescence; promote lateral bud growth; Skoog and Miller — tissue culture); Ethylene (gaseous; fruit ripening; promotes senescence; abscission; triple response in peas; produced in response to stress; flooding induces in rice causing stem elongation); Abscisic acid (ABA; stress hormone — drought, salinity; stomatal closure; dormancy; inhibits germination; "dormin"); Brassinosteroids (promote cell elongation; similar to gibberellins)
- Vernalisation: cold treatment required for flowering in certain plants (winter wheat, biennials); prevents premature flowering; meristematic regions perceive cold
- Photoperiodism: effect of day length (actually night length) on flowering; Short-day plants (SDP; flower when NIGHT exceeds critical length; rice, soybean, Chrysanthemum, tobacco, Xanthium; actually long-night plants); Long-day plants (LDP; flower when NIGHT is shorter than critical; wheat, radish, oat, sugar beet; long-day actually); Day-neutral plants (flowering not dependent on photoperiod; tomato, maize, sunflower, cucumber); phytochrome (photoreceptor; Pr=inactive, red absorbs; Pfr=active, far-red absorbs; Pfr promotes flowering in LDP; inhibits in SDP; Pfr reverts to Pr in darkness)

**Ch 16: Digestion and Absorption**
- GI tract structure (from mouth to anus): buccal cavity → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum+jejunum+ileum) → large intestine (caecum+appendix+colon+rectum) → anus; accessory: salivary glands, liver, gall bladder, pancreas
- Digestion: Buccal cavity: salivary amylase (ptyalin; starch → maltose + dextrin; pH 6.8; inactivated by gastric HCl); Stomach: HCl (kills bacteria; activates pepsinogen → pepsin; pH ~2); pepsin (protein → proteoses + peptones); gastric lipase (minor; fat → fatty acids + glycerol); mucus (protects); Rennin (infants; casein → paracasein); Small intestine: pancreatic juice (trypsinogen → trypsin by enterokinase; chymotrypsinogen → chymotrypsin by trypsin; procarboxypeptidase → carboxypeptidase; pancreatic amylase; pancreatic lipase; ribonuclease; deoxyribonuclease; bile salts emulsify fats); intestinal juice/succus entericus (maltase; sucrase/invertase; lactase; peptidases; intestinal lipase; enterokinase activates trypsinogen)
- Absorption: ileum (highest; villi + microvilli = brush border; crypts of Lieberkühn; lacteals = lymph capillaries in villus core; absorb fats → thoracic duct; glucose, amino acids, water-soluble vitamins → portal blood); Duodenum (iron, Ca²⁺); Jejunum (most nutrients); Large intestine (water, electrolytes, some vitamins; gut flora produce vitamin K, biotin, B12; no digestion); Stomach (alcohol, some water, aspirin)
- Disorders: jaundice (bilirubin in blood; yellow skin/sclera; liver disease, haemolysis, bile duct obstruction); vomiting (reverse peristalsis); diarrhoea (rapid passage; reduced absorption; bacteria, virus, parasites); constipation (slow movement; hard faeces; dehydration); PEM (protein-energy malnutrition: marasmus = calorie + protein deficiency; kwashiorkor = protein deficiency after weaning; clinical signs)

**Ch 17: Breathing and Exchange of Gases**
- Respiratory organs: nasal cavity (filter, warm, humidify); pharynx; larynx (voice box; Adam's apple); trachea (C-rings of cartilage; pseudostratified ciliated epithelium; cilia remove debris); bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli (300 million; 60-80 m² surface; one cell thick; rich capillary supply; type I pneumocytes = gas exchange; type II = surfactant)
- Mechanics: diaphragm (dome-shaped muscle; main muscle of inspiration; descends on contraction; increases thoracic volume); external intercostals (elevate ribs; increase thoracic volume); inspiration = active (muscles contract; volume increases; pressure decreases; air in); expiration = passive (muscles relax; elastic recoil; volume decreases; air out); forced expiration uses internal intercostals + abdominal muscles
- Lung volumes (memorise ALL): TV (tidal volume = normal quiet breath = 500 mL); IRV (inspiratory reserve = extra that can be inhaled = 3000 mL); ERV (expiratory reserve = extra that can be exhaled = 1100 mL); RV (residual volume = cannot be exhaled = 1200 mL); IC (inspiratory capacity = TV+IRV = 3500); FRC (functional residual capacity = ERV+RV = 2300); VC (vital capacity = TV+IRV+ERV = 4600 mL); TLC (total lung capacity = VC+RV = 5800 mL); dead space = 150 mL (trachea + bronchi; no gas exchange)
- Gas exchange: partial pressure determines direction (O₂: alveoli 104 mmHg → blood 40 mmHg → tissues 5–40 mmHg; CO₂: tissues 45 mmHg → blood 40 mmHg → alveoli 35 mmHg); Fick's law (rate ∝ surface area × concentration gradient × solubility / thickness)
- O₂ transport: oxyhaemoglobin (98.5%; haemoglobin binds O₂ cooperatively; sigmoid dissociation curve; Bohr effect: low pH, high CO₂, high temperature → right shift → more O₂ released to tissues); dissolved in plasma (1.5%)
- CO₂ transport: bicarbonate (70%; CO₂+H₂O → carbonic anhydrase → H₂CO₃ → H⁺+HCO₃⁻; chloride shift = Hamburger shift; HCO₃⁻ out of RBC → Cl⁻ in); carbaminohaemoglobin (23%; CO₂ binds globin protein, not haem); dissolved (7%)
- Respiratory volumes in disorders: asthma (reduced VC, TLC; increased RV); emphysema (increased RV, TLC; reduced VC); spirometry

**Ch 18: Body Fluids and Circulation ⭐**
- Blood: plasma (~55%; water 91-92%; proteins: albumin (osmotic pressure), globulins (immunity), fibrinogen (clotting), prothrombin; inorganic salts; nutrients; waste; hormones; gases); formed elements (~45%): RBC (7.5μm diameter; biconcave disc; no nucleus in mature; 4-5 million/μL; life 120 days; haemoglobin 12-16 g/dL; produced in bone marrow=erythropoiesis; destroyed in spleen); WBC (7-8000/μL; nucleated; Granulocytes: neutrophil=60-65% phagocyte; eosinophil=2-3% allergy/parasite; basophil=0.5-1% heparin histamine; Agranulocytes: lymphocyte=20-25% immune; monocyte=6-8% phagocyte→macrophage); Platelets=thrombocytes (2.5 lakh/μL; no nucleus; produce thromboplastin; life 7-10 days)
- Blood groups: ABO (Karl Landsteiner 1900; IA=antigen A, IB=antigen B, ii=no antigen; codominance IA and IB; ii recessive; A (IAIA or IAi), B (IBIB or IBi), AB (IAIB, universal recipient), O (ii, universal donor); antibodies in plasma: anti-B if group A, anti-A if group B, none if AB, both anti-A and anti-B if O); Rh factor (Rh+ has Rh antigen; Rh- has no antigen but makes anti-Rh antibodies after exposure; erythroblastosis foetalis: Rh- mother with Rh+ foetus; first pregnancy safe; second pregnancy: maternal anti-Rh antibodies cross placenta → destroy foetal RBCs → haemolytic disease; prevention: anti-D immunoglobulin injection to Rh- mother within 72 hours of delivery)
- Coagulation: platelet plug formation → coagulation cascade → prothrombin → thrombin (thrombokinase/thromboplastin + Ca²⁺) → fibrinogen → fibrin (clot); Haemophilia A: factor VIII deficiency; Haemophilia B: factor IX deficiency; Vitamin K required for synthesis of several clotting factors (II, VII, IX, X)
- Human heart: location (thoracic cavity; between lungs; pericardium surrounds; double-walled); 4 chambers (right atrium receives deoxygenated blood from SVC, IVC, coronary sinus; right ventricle pumps to pulmonary artery; left atrium receives oxygenated from 4 pulmonary veins; left ventricle pumps to aorta; thickest wall); valves: tricuspid (RA-RV; 3 cusps); bicuspid/mitral (LA-LV; 2 cusps); semilunar (aortic=at aortic root; pulmonary=at pulmonary trunk; 3 cusps each); SA node (sinoatrial node; right atrium; pacemaker; 70-80 impulses/min; NSR); AV node (atrioventricular; at junction of RA and RV; delays impulse 0.1s to allow ventricular filling); Bundle of His (interventricular septum; splits into left and right bundles); Purkinje fibres (spread impulse throughout ventricles); automaticity — heart can beat without nervous control (myogenic)
- Cardiac cycle (0.8 s at 75 bpm): Joint diastole (0.4 s; all chambers relaxed; AV valves open; semilunar closed; passive filling); Atrial systole (0.1 s; atria contract; squeeze remaining blood into ventricles; SAN fires; AV valves still open); Ventricular systole (0.3 s; ventricles contract; pressure rises; AV valves close → first heart sound "LUB"; pressure exceeds arterial → semilunar valves open → blood ejected; then semilunar valves close → second heart sound "DUB"); SV = 70 mL; CO = HR × SV = 75 × 70 = ~5 L/min; Starling's law (stroke volume increases with increased venous return/preload)
- ECG: P wave (atrial depolarization; ~0.1s); PR interval (AV node delay; 0.12-0.2s; prolonged = 1st degree AV block); QRS complex (ventricular depolarization; ~0.1s; small = poor ventricular muscle; wide = bundle branch block); ST segment (ventricular depolarization complete; elevated = MI; depressed = ischaemia); T wave (ventricular repolarization; inverted = ischaemia); QT interval; RR interval (= 60/HR seconds); normal HR = 60-100 bpm
- Double circulation: systemic (left heart → tissues → right heart) + pulmonary (right heart → lungs → left heart); separation ensures oxygenated and deoxygenated blood never mix (unlike 3-chambered amphibian heart)

**Ch 19: Excretory Products and Elimination ⭐**
- Types of nitrogenous waste: ammonotelism (aquatic organisms; NH₃; highly toxic; needs lots of water; most fish, tadpoles, aquatic insects); ureotelism (mammals, adult amphibians, marine fishes, some terrestrial arthropods; urea; less toxic; requires less water; ornithine cycle in liver); uricotelism (birds, reptiles, insects, land snails; uric acid = least toxic; least water needed; semi-solid paste; important for desert animals and shelled eggs)
- Human excretory organs: kidneys (primary; paired; retroperitoneal; right lower than left due to liver); skin (sweat glands; water, salt, urea, lactic acid); lungs (CO₂, water vapour); liver (bilirubin from haemoglobin; bile; detoxification)
- Kidney structure: renal cortex (outer; Bowman's capsules, PCT, DCT); renal medulla (inner; loops of Henle, collecting ducts; 8-18 renal pyramids; renal papillae); renal pelvis → ureter → urinary bladder → urethra; blood supply: renal artery → afferent arteriole → glomerulus → efferent arteriole → peritubular capillaries → vasa recta (around loop of Henle) → renal vein
- Nephron (functional unit): Malpighian corpuscle = Bowman's capsule (cup-shaped; podocytes with pedicels and filtration slits) + glomerulus (tuft of capillaries; high pressure due to afferent wider than efferent); PCT (proximal convoluted tubule; brush border; reabsorbs ~70% water, all glucose/amino acids, most ions by active/facilitated transport; selective, non-threshold); Loop of Henle (descending limb — permeable to water but not solutes; water leaves; hyperosmotic filtrate; ascending limb — impermeable to water; solutes pumped out; creates medullary osmotic gradient; countercurrent multiplier); DCT (distal convoluted tubule; fine-tuning; hormonal regulation; aldosterone → Na⁺ reabsorption, K⁺ secretion; H⁺/NH₃ secretion); Collecting duct (ADH → aquaporins → water reabsorption; concentration of urine; medullary collecting duct → renal pelvis); Juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA: juxtaglomerular cells in afferent arteriole + macula densa in DCT; renin secretion when BP drops)
- Urine formation (3 steps): Glomerular filtration (GFR=125 mL/min=180 L/day; driven by hydrostatic pressure 60 mmHg minus oncotic pressure 32 mmHg minus capsular pressure 18 mmHg = net filtration 10 mmHg; filtered: water, glucose, amino acids, urea, creatinine, ions; NOT filtered: plasma proteins, RBCs, WBCs); Tubular reabsorption (178.5 L recovered; 99% water; all glucose (up to threshold ~180 mg/dL), all amino acids; 80% Na⁺+Cl⁻; 50% urea; HCO₃⁻; selective); Tubular secretion (additional removal: H⁺ ions; K⁺; NH₃; drugs: penicillin, creatinine; maintains blood pH)
- Hormonal regulation: ADH/vasopressin (posterior pituitary; increased plasma osmolarity → ADH released → aquaporin-2 channels in DCT/collecting duct → water reabsorption increases → dilute urine stops; excess water → no ADH → dilute urine; diabetes insipidus: no ADH → copious dilute urine 10-20 L/day); Aldosterone (adrenal cortex; angiotensin II → aldosterone → Na⁺ reabsorption from DCT/CD → K⁺ and H⁺ secretion → water follows Na⁺ → BP increases); Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS): decreased renal blood pressure/Na⁺ → JG cells → renin → angiotensinogen (liver) → angiotensin I → ACE (lung) → angiotensin II → aldosterone + direct vasoconstriction → BP rises; ANF (atrial natriuretic factor; secreted by cardiac atria when blood volume high → promotes Na⁺ excretion → reduces BP; opposes RAAS); Parathyroid hormone (PTH: increases Ca²⁺ reabsorption from DCT)
- Countercurrent mechanism: loop of Henle creates interstitial hyperosmolarity (from ~300 mOsm in cortex to ~1200 mOsm in deep medulla); descending loop (osmosis out → filtrate becomes hyperosmolar); ascending loop (active transport of Na⁺, Cl⁻ out; impermeable to water → filtrate becomes hypoosmolar); vasa recta (countercurrent exchange; blood flowing down gains solutes/loses water; flowing up loses solutes/gains water; net effect: solutes recycled, water removed to blood; maintains interstitial gradient)
- Micturition: urinary bladder stores up to 700-800 mL; stretch receptors trigger micturition reflex (parasympathetic → detrusor muscle contraction + internal sphincter relaxation → voiding); voluntary control of external sphincter (pudendal nerve; learned)
- Disorders: Glomerulonephritis (inflammation; protein in urine); nephrotic syndrome (massive proteinuria > 3.5g/day; oedema; hypoalbuminaemia); kidney stones (calcium oxalate; uric acid; struvite; obstruction); UTI; renal failure (acute: reversible; chronic: irreversible; CKD stages 1-5); dialysis (haemodialysis: blood passes through artificial kidney/dialyser; semipermeable membrane against dialysate; removes urea, creatinine, excess ions; 3-4 hours 3× per week; peritoneal dialysis: peritoneum as membrane; dialysate infused into abdominal cavity; removed after 4-6 hours); kidney transplant

**Ch 20: Locomotion and Movement**
- Types: Amoeboid movement (pseudopodia; cytoskeletal rearrangement; macrophages, WBCs); Ciliary movement (fallopian tubes; trachea; remove particles); Muscular movement (locomotion, heart, GI)
- Muscle types: Skeletal (striated; voluntary; fast; fatiguable; multinucleate; attached to bone via tendons); Smooth (unstriated; involuntary; slow; non-fatiguable; uninucleate; walls of hollow organs); Cardiac (striated; involuntary; non-fatiguable; uninucleate; intercalated discs with gap junctions; inherent rhythmicity)
- Skeletal muscle structure: muscle → fascicles → fibres → myofibrils → sarcomeres (functional unit; from Z-line to Z-line); Sarcomere components: thick filament = myosin (globular head with ATPase activity; binds actin; heavy and light chains; M-line connects); thin filament = actin (F-actin = G-actin polymers; tropomyosin covers binding sites; troponin C binds Ca²⁺, troponin I inhibits binding, troponin T binds tropomyosin); A-band (dark; myosin + some actin overlap; does NOT change length); I-band (light; actin only; SHORTENS during contraction); H-zone (myosin only; in centre of A-band; SHORTENS); Z-line (connects thin filaments)
- Sliding filament theory (Huxley & Hanson 1954): Ca²⁺ released from SR (sarcoplasmic reticulum; stimulated by action potential → T-tubules) → binds troponin C → conformational change → tropomyosin moves → actin binding sites exposed → myosin head binds actin (cross-bridge) → myosin head swings (power stroke; uses ATP) → actin moves toward M-line → H-zone and I-band shorten → sarcomere shortens → ATP binds → myosin detaches → repeats; Ca²⁺ pumped back into SR → tropomyosin covers sites → muscle relaxes
- Rigor mortis: after death, ATP production stops; Ca²⁺ leaks; myosin cannot detach from actin → stiffness; resolves as proteins denature 24-72h after death
- Skeleton: Axial (skull, vertebral column, sternum, ribs); Appendicular (pectoral girdle + upper limbs + pelvic girdle + lower limbs); bones (206 in adult; haversian system in compact bone; spongy/cancellous bone with trabeculae; red marrow in flat bones + ends of long bones = haemopoiesis; yellow marrow in shaft = fat); joints: fibrous (immovable; sutures in skull); cartilaginous (slightly movable; intervertebral discs; pubic symphysis); synovial (freely movable; synovial fluid = lubricant; hyaline cartilage on articular surfaces; types: ball and socket=hip/shoulder; hinge=elbow/knee; pivot=atlas-axis; gliding=carpals; saddle=thumb CMC; condyloid=wrist)
- Disorders: Arthritis (inflammation of joints; rheumatoid arthritis — autoimmune; osteoarthritis — wear and tear); Osteoporosis (reduced bone mass; oestrogen deficiency; Ca²⁺ deficiency; common in post-menopausal women); Gout (uric acid crystals in joints; big toe most common; purine metabolism disorder); Muscular dystrophy (genetic; progressive muscle wasting; Duchenne — X-linked; dystrophin protein absent); Myasthenia gravis (autoimmune; antibodies against acetylcholine receptors at NMJ; progressive muscle weakness)

**Ch 21: Neural Control and Coordination ⭐**
- Neuron (functional unit): dendrites (receive input; increase surface area; no myelin); cell body/soma (nucleus; nissl bodies=rough ER; metabolic centre; axon hillock); axon (single; conducts away from cell body; myelinated: myelin sheath from Schwann cells; nodes of Ranvier between sheaths; faster conduction; unmyelinated: slow conduction); classified: unipolar (embryonic), bipolar (retinal cells, olfactory), multipolar (motor neurons, Purkinje cells)
- Resting membrane potential: −70 mV (inside negative); maintained by Na⁺-K⁺ pump (3Na⁺ out, 2K⁺ in per ATP; electrogenic); K⁺ leak channels; high K⁺ inside, high Na⁺ outside; Cl⁻ outside; large anions inside
- Action potential: Depolarisation (stimulus → voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open → Na⁺ rushes in → membrane depolarises to +40 mV; all-or-none; threshold ~−55 mV); Repolarisation (Na⁺ channels inactivate; K⁺ channels open → K⁺ rushes out → membrane repolarises); Hyperpolarisation (K⁺ channels slow to close; briefly more negative than −70 mV = refractory period); propagation: in myelinated nerve = saltatory conduction (jumps from node to node; much faster; up to 120 m/s); unmyelinated continuous conduction (slow; 0.5–2 m/s)
- Synapse: presynaptic membrane → synaptic cleft (20 nm) → postsynaptic membrane; synaptic vesicles (contain neurotransmitters); action potential → Ca²⁺ influx via voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels → vesicle fusion → exocytosis → NT binds receptor; excitatory (EPSP: depolarises; e.g. acetylcholine at NMJ; glutamate in CNS); inhibitory (IPSP: hyperpolarises; e.g. GABA, glycine)
- Neurotransmitters: Acetylcholine (NMJ; autonomic ganglia; parasympathetic; Alzheimer's disease deficiency); Dopamine (reward pathway; Parkinson's disease deficiency; schizophrenia excess); Serotonin (mood; sleep; depression deficiency — SSRIs work here); Noradrenaline (sympathetic postganglionic; stress); GABA (main inhibitory in CNS); Glutamate (main excitatory in CNS); Substance P (pain); Endorphins (pain relief)
- CNS: Brain (protected by skull + meninges: dura mater + arachnoid mater + pia mater; CSF in subarachnoid space and ventricles — cushion, nutrient); Cerebrum (largest; 2 hemispheres; corpus callosum connects; grey matter = cortex; white matter inside; lobes: frontal=motor, voluntary movement, speech Broca's area, executive function, personality; parietal=sensory, somatosensory cortex, body awareness; temporal=hearing, memory Wernicke's area, smell, face recognition; occipital=vision; functional areas: motor cortex, sensory cortex, visual cortex, auditory cortex, association areas); Diencephalon (thalamus: relay station, sensory filter; hypothalamus: homeostasis, temperature, hunger, thirst, circadian rhythm, ADH, oxytocin released by posterior pituitary, control of anterior pituitary via releasing/inhibiting hormones); Brainstem (midbrain: visual and auditory reflexes, RAS=alertness; pons: cranial nerve nuclei, respiration, sleep; medulla oblongata: vital centres — cardiac, vasomotor, respiratory, deglutition, vomiting); Cerebellum (posterior; motor coordination; balance; posture; fine motor control; damage → ataxia, dysmetria)
- Spinal cord: from foramen magnum to L2; central canal; grey matter (H-shaped; dorsal horn = sensory; ventral horn = motor) + white matter (ascending sensory tracts; descending motor tracts); reflex arc (receptor → sensory neuron → integrating centre spinal cord → motor neuron → effector; knee-jerk monosynaptic; withdrawal polysynaptic; spinal cord controls reflex; brain receives copy)
- Eye: layers (fibrous: sclera white + cornea transparent; vascular: choroid + ciliary body + iris; nervous: retina); cornea (no blood vessels; transparent; refracts light); pupil (opening in iris; diameter controlled by sphincter pupillae [circular; constriction; parasympathetic] and dilator pupillae [radial; dilation; sympathetic]); lens (biconvex; elastic; zonular fibres + ciliary muscle for accommodation: near = ciliary contracts → zonules relax → lens curves → increases power); aqueous humour (between cornea and lens); vitreous humour (between lens and retina); retina (photoreceptors: rods 120 million [scotopsin protein + retinal=rhodopsin; dim light; no colour; peripheral vision], cones 6-7 million [iodopsin; 3 types S=430nm B, M=530nm G, L=560nm R; colour vision; fovea centralis=highest cone density=sharpest vision]; bipolar cells → ganglion cells → optic nerve; macula lutea = yellow; fovea within; blind spot = optic disc = no photoreceptors; optic chiasm = nasal fibres cross)
- Ear: External (pinna; auditory canal; ceruminous glands=wax; tympanic membrane=eardrum); Middle (3 ossicles: malleus=hammer (attached to tympanic membrane), incus=anvil, stapes=stirrup (attached to oval window); Eustachian tube (connects middle ear to pharynx; equalises pressure); Inner (bony labyrinth filled with perilymph; membranous labyrinth filled with endolymph; Cochlea: hearing organ; 2.5 turns; 3 chambers: scala vestibuli (perilymph) + scala media/cochlear duct (endolymph; organ of Corti) + scala tympani (perilymph); organ of Corti: basilar membrane (stiffness varies: high frequency at base, low at apex), hair cells (inner=single row=actual receptor; outer=amplify; stereocilia), tectorial membrane (gelatinous; stereocilia embedded); sound: oval window vibration → perilymph → basilar membrane vibrates → hair cells bend → mechanotransduction → nerve impulses; Vestibular apparatus: utricle + saccule (linear acceleration; gravity; otoliths=CaCO₃ crystals on macula; maculae sense head position); 3 semicircular canals (angular acceleration; 3 planes; crista ampullaris at ampulla; cupula displaced by endolymph movement)

## SECTION 7 — 24-WEEK STUDY PLAN

- Trick: make comparison table — Kingdom vs Key Feature vs Example per row

**Weeks 5–6 | Chemistry: Physical I (Basic Concepts + Atomic Structure + Chemical Bonding)**

- Atomic Structure: Bohr model; quantum numbers; orbital shapes; Aufbau/Pauli/Hund
- Chemical Bonding: hybridization; MOT bond order; VSEPR shapes
- Daily: 10 numericals + 10 MCQs

**Weeks 7–8 | Physics: Mechanics (Ch 1–10)**
- Kinematics: all 5 equations; projectile; relative velocity

- Work-Energy: spring problems; elastic/inelastic collisions
- Rotational: MI of 6 bodies; parallel + perpendicular axis theorems; angular momentum conservation
- Gravitation: variation of g; escape velocity √(2gR); orbital velocity √(gR)
- Daily: 5 FBD problems + 5 MCQs

### Phase 2 — Core Building (Weeks 9–16)

**Week 9–10 | Biology: Plant Physiology (Class XI Ch 11–15)**
- PHOTOSYNTHESIS (HIGH YIELD): Draw Z-scheme from memory daily
  - PS II → P680 → H₂O photolysis → e⁻ → PQ → Cyt b6f → PC → PS I → P700 → Fd → NADPH
  - Calvin cycle: CO₂ + RuBP (5C) →RuBisCO→ 2×3-PGA (3C) → G3P → RuBP regenerated
  - C4 (Hatch-Slack): PEP carboxylase in mesophyll; OAA→malate; decarboxylated in bundle sheath
- RESPIRATION (HIGH YIELD):
  - Glycolysis net: 2 ATP + 2 NADH + 2 pyruvate
  - Krebs (×2): 6 NADH + 2 FADH₂ + 2 GTP + 4 CO₂
  - ETC: NADH→2.5 ATP; FADH₂→1.5 ATP; Total ~30–32 ATP per glucose

**Week 11 | Biology: Human Physiology I (Digestion + Breathing + Circulation)**
- CIRCULATION (3–5 Qs expected):
  - SA node (pacemaker) → AV node → Bundle of His → Purkinje fibres
  - Cardiac cycle: 0.8s; SV=70mL; CO=5L/min
  - ECG: P (atrial depol) + QRS (ventricular depol) + T (ventricular repol)
  - Blood groups: ABO (codominance); Rh factor; erythroblastosis foetalis
- BREATHING: Lung volumes (TV=500; IRV=3000; ERV=1100; RV=1200 mL); VC=4600; TLC=5800
- O₂ transport: oxyhaemoglobin 97% + dissolved 3%; Bohr effect; sigmoid dissociation curve
- CO₂: bicarbonate 70% + carbamino-Hb 23% + dissolved 7%

**Week 12 | Biology: Human Physiology II (Excretion + Neural + Hormones)**
- NEPHRON (3–5 Qs): Draw + label from memory every day this week
  - GFR=125 mL/min=180 L/day filtered; 178.5 L reabsorbed → 1.5 L urine
  - ADH: water reabsorption; Aldosterone: Na⁺ retention; RAAS: BP control
- HORMONES (3–6 Qs): Master the gland→hormone→function→disorder table
- NEURAL: Resting −70mV; action potential; synapse; Eye and Ear structure

**Weeks 13–14 | Chemistry: Organic Chemistry (ALL chapters)**
- Week 13: Mechanisms — free radical halogenation; Markovnikov; SN1 vs SN2; E1 vs E2

**Weeks 15–16 | Biology: GENETICS + MOLECULAR BIOLOGY (24% of Biology)**

- Week 16: Molecular Biology (14% of Biology — SINGLE HIGHEST-YIELD chapter in all of NEET)
